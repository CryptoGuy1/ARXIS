v14: editorial pass on structure, Summary and style

No experiment was re-run and no reported number changed. This commit changes the
shape of the paper and the way it reads, in response to an editorial review.

1. Structure. All nine experiments now precede the Discussion; Experiments 8 and
   9 previously sat after it. The two eventization analyses (alarm episodes,
   hazard episodes) moved from Model Application into Results, because they
   reinterpret the perturbation sweep and in one case reverse a model ordering.
   Model Application became Engineering Implications and now carries only what
   follows from the results. Sections renamed: Prior Research Review ->
   Related Work and Positioning; Proposed Method -> Methodology; Experimental
   Results and Discussion -> Results; Conclusion -> Conclusions.

   Tables renumbered with their content: proposed screen 14 -> 16, alarm
   episodes 15 -> 14, hazard episodes 16 -> 15.

   Experiments 4, 5 and 7 are grouped into one supporting subsection, keeping
   only the finding each contributes to the rest of the paper, with the detail
   pointed at S3, S4 and S7.

2. Summary cut from 565 to 404 words. The reject-option and raw-fault findings
   are one sentence; the "one in eleven" discussion is gone from the abstract
   and kept where it is derived. It had also called the 0.011 / 0.245
   comparison "reconstruction error" when it is the normalized anomaly feature.

3. Title: ARXIS: Safety-Oriented Evaluation of Learned Gas-Monitoring Models
   Under Class Exclusion and Sensor Degradation. Propagated to the supporting
   information and the README.

4. Style. Eleven rhetorical constructions the review flagged are removed
   ("worth stating precisely", "three things follow", "that is the point",
   "the objection is reasonable", and the rest), replaced with result ->
   qualification -> implication. "rather than" fell from 93 uses to 55.

   Two errors surfaced while rewriting: the Conclusions said "Four findings
   follow" above five, and its scope paragraph still claimed no reject option
   was tested and that all degradation was in standardized features, both made
   false by Experiments 8 and 9.

5. Repeated caveats consolidated to one definitive treatment each. The overlap
   and dependence caveat was stated in full twice; the Limitations copy is now
   a cross-reference.

6. Tables 14 and 15 condensed to their informative rows, with the complete
   tables added as Tables S11 and S12 under a new supporting Section S13,
   generated from the result files rather than transcribed. Some condensed rows
   name a count of models instead of one model; four new gates check those
   groupings against v3_episode_metrics.csv.

7. Body length: 23,223 -> 20,621 words, a cut of 11.2%. The review asked for
   25 to 30%. Reaching that from here means deleting the qualifications four
   earlier review rounds required (bound-versus-estimate language, constructed
   target declarations, class-exclusion-is-not-domain-shift, descriptive-counts
   framing, persistence-rule sensitivity), which trades one reviewer's objection
   for another's. Two further reductions are available as decisions rather than
   edits and are set out in the change review.

   Word bands in verify_v13.py retightened to the new structure so they bind.

8. Repaired a defect the previous commit introduced. The Kumar et al. (2024)
   reference was moved to keep the reference list alphabetical using
   t.index("Laberge"), which matched an in-text citation in the Introduction
   rather than the reference entry. The full reference was spliced into the
   middle of a sentence about alarm burden and shipped that way, with every
   suite passing, because nothing checked the body for reference-shaped text.
   Sentence restored, reference relocated, and three gates added: no
   reference-style entry in body prose, no bare DOI or URL in body prose, and
   no citation without a matching entry in either direction. The gates were
   checked by re-injecting the fault.

9. Added Lima et al. (2026), "Autoencoders for Early Kick Detection: Improving
   Safety in Offshore Oilwell Drilling", SPE J. online 10 June 2026,
   SPE-231854-PA, from the DSEA collection record. Volume, issue and pages are
   not quoted: the article page is behind a bot check that was not worked
   around, so the entry uses the online-first form and needs completing if the
   paper has since been assigned to an issue.

Nine verification gates rewritten to follow reworded prose; each still checks
the same substantive claim.
