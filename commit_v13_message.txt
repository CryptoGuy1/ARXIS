v13: correct four evaluation defects, add episode-level and block-bootstrap evidence

Four defects in the evaluation code were found and corrected. Every number in
the manuscript was regenerated after the fixes; none was edited by hand.

1. Block-wise holdout applied the embargo on one side of the test band only,
   so windows immediately before each test block could still overlap it in raw
   time. The embargo is now two-sided (retrain/raw_pipeline.py). Minimum
   separation is now 21 window starts on every seed.

2. The LSTM autoencoder used for the anomaly path was fitted, scaler and
   checkpoint both, on all NoGas data including the test partition. It is now
   refitted inside each training partition (partition_anomaly / train_anomaly_on
   in retrain/raw_pipeline.py). Held-out ROC-AUC falls from 0.9928 +/- 0.0053
   to 0.9251 +/- 0.0618 and the false-positive rate rises from 0.0000 to
   0.1101 +/- 0.0992. The reported figures are the corrected ones.

3. The CUSUM baseline carried its statistic across class-block boundaries
   (retrain/new_baselines.py). It now resets at each block change. Its false-
   alarm rate moves from 0 to 0.1861.

4. Calibration used an i.i.d. bootstrap and a random permutation split
   (retrain/run_calib_actions_v2.py). Both now respect temporal blocking:
   moving-block bootstrap, and a contiguous per-class calibration tail with a
   20-window embargo. The fitted temperature reverses from 0.787 +/- 0.156
   (under-confident) to 1.423 +/- 0.202 (over-confident); the manuscript's
   calibration discussion was rewritten accordingly.

Uncertainty treatment
  - Moving-block bootstrap at L = 20, 100, 316 (retrain/run_block_bootstrap.py).
  - Binomial bounds are labelled independence-reference bounds, not exact
    confidence bounds.
  - Disjoint-support correction: only 31/632 hazardous and 15/316 clean test
    windows have pairwise-disjoint raw support, which moves a bound on a zero
    from 0.47 percent to 9.21 percent and from 0.94 percent to 18.10 percent.

New evidence
  - Episode-level hazard metrics and detection latency
    (retrain/run_episode_metrics.py, Table 14).
  - Eventized alarm burden, compression 5.5x to 316x
    (retrain/run_alarm_episodes.py, Table 13).
  - ROPE-width sweep at +/- 0.5, 1 and 2 points (retrain/run_rope_sensitivity.py).
  - Per-class reconstruction error under the partition-local anomaly path
    (retrain/run_anomaly_by_class.py).

Numerical contradictions corrected
  117 vs 128; the stale 0.0098 / 0.3405 figure caption; 2.48 vs 2.42;
  "203" to "about 200"; "factor of 40", which was a ratio of bounds and not a

Second round: the five-part adversarial review
----------------------------------------------
One promise the paper made and had not kept, and one artifact it cited and could
not produce, were both found by that review and are fixed here.

The eventized alarm result is collapsed under one persistence rule (on-delay 3,
off-delay 15) and the paper used it to reorder the models, while saying only that
"the effect of that choice is bounded below" and never bounding it.
retrain/run_persistence_sweep.py now re-eventizes the same alarm-grade flag
sequences at 25 on-delay x off-delay settings with nothing refitted, and reports
Kendall tau against the deployed rule plus every pairwise reversal (Table S7,
new Supplementary Section S9).

  - The single-channel reordering survives all 25 settings: the shallow tree
    annunciates strictly less than the cost-weighted policy at 20, equal at 5,
    and more at none.
  - The secondary four-vs-seven-channel observation reverses at 2 settings, both
    at an off-delay of 60 windows, and is now reported with that qualification.
  - The claim that every condition falls within or near the EEMUA reference once
    eventized does NOT survive. At noise sigma = 0.5 the grid reaches 109
    episodes per hour, and at sigma = 0.3 the deployed rule reports zero
    annunciated episodes from every comparator while a shorter on-delay reports
    up to 50 per hour. The claim is now stated as conditional on the rule, in the
    body and in a new Limitations entry.

v3_metric_cost.json was cited in Supplementary Section S8 but no script in the
repository produced it. retrain/run_metric_cost.py now does, and the regenerated
figures replace the old ones: 0.455 ms against 0.290 ms, ratio 1.57x, previously
0.478 / 0.264 / 1.81x.

Wording corrections, each gated:
  - The missed-hazard bound read as an estimate ("consistent with a true rate
    near one in eleven"); it is an upper bound and now says so in both places.
  - The ten-comparator Pearson intervals were labelled 95% confidence intervals;
    ten author-chosen models sharing one corpus are not a sample from a
    population, so they are now exploratory reference intervals.
  - The class-disjoint experiment now states plainly that no comparator has a
    reject option and that it is a class-exclusion, forced-classification stress
    test rather than open-set detection.
  - Table 14's ten episodes per condition are declared descriptive counts:
    7/10 carries a one-sided 95% lower bound of 0.39 and even 10/10 only
    establishes detection above 0.74.
  - Run-level SDs are declared computational rather than physical replication;
    the Bayesian correlated t-test is offered as a sensitivity analysis over an
    assumed dependence structure; bootstrap agreement is declared robustness to
    the analysis choice, not independent confirmation.
  - Limitations said dropout was "zeroing of the standardized channel features"
    while Experiment 6 said it was the training mean. Both are true under a
    training-partition standard scaler; the first reads as an electrical zero,
    which it is not, and now says so.

Reproducibility:
  - Appendix A's prose artifact list is replaced by Table A-1, a provenance table
    mapping every reported object to its driver, result file, protocol and make
    target.
  - The four superseded drivers (run_exp1_real, run_exp3_loco,
    run_exp4_calibration, run_expzoo) carry deprecation headers and are declared
    in the appendix. Their headers described the network in reinforcement-
    learning terms although it is trained by cost-weighted cross-entropy.
  - Yao et al. (2024) added, at the sentence on joint drift and unknown-class
    treatment that previously carried no citation.

Third round: the complete must-fix pre-submission plan
------------------------------------------------------
The Table 10 bounds were computed from an average, not from a count. The stated
rule is the worst case across partitions, but the code took the mean missed-
hazard RATE over five seeds, multiplied by 1,581 and rounded that product into a
pseudo-count. Where the seeds agree the two agree; on this corpus they do not.

  Smoke / multilayer perceptron   misses per partition 4, 23, 30, 216, 234
                                  bound 7.49%  ->  16.35%
  Mixture / shallow tree          misses per partition 0, 0, 489, 489, 988
                                  bound 26.71% ->  64.51%
  Mixture / k-nearest neighbors   24, 28, 31, 38, 80    3.28% -> 6.06%
  Mixture / multilayer perceptron 0, 0, 0, 24, 54       1.53% -> 4.27%

run_final.py now writes v3_loco_perseed.csv with the integer count each partition
produced, run_partition_bounds.py reads it, and Table 10 carries the counts as a
column of their own. The shallow tree is flawless on two partitions and
catastrophic on three; an average is a poor summary of that, which is the reason
the counts are printed.

Dependent claims, all updated:
  - the headline separation is now a factor of about 86, not 40, gated against
    both the exact ratio and the ratio recomputed from the printed figures;
  - "ten of eleven pass a miss-rate screen at or below 3.3%" was false under the
    stated method. Eight of eleven hold worst-partition bounds at or below 3.3%,
    and the word "pass" is gone from the paper.

rho disagreed with the paper's own counts. Eq. 11 said 0.202; 4,900 and 1,264
give 0.20506. The cause was a hard-coded (1264, 4980) in the comparison driver,
and 4,980 is not a number the pipeline produces under any seed.
run_split_counts.py derives rho from the partition and checks it across all 35
protocol seeds; run_bayes_recompute.py redoes the posteriors at the corrected
value from the stored per-run accuracies, so nothing was retrained to change an
arithmetic constant. Seven of ten equivalence probabilities moved, none by more
than 0.003, and no conclusion changed.

Errors of fact:
  - the Conclusion said the early/late protocol cost three models "between 27 and
    29 points"; Table 7 gives 41.2, 37.9 and 29.5;
  - Fig. 18 and Table 15 were cited and do not exist (15 figures, 14 tables);
  - Table 5 said "exact upper bound" where the Methods explain it is not exact;
  - Dehnaw et al. (2024) was cited for local language models; that paper is about
    edge gas detection, and Gemma Team (2025) now carries the claim;
  - the supporting information still had the old title, including the phrase
    "Under Distribution Shift" that the manuscript disclaims.

Eq. 2 used one symbol for two objects: a*(Perfume) = {1,2} made Eq. 3 take the
log-probability of a set. Now Eq. 2a gives the single-valued training target y(g)
and Eq. 2b the acceptable set A(g), which is what the code always did. Action 2
is never a positive training target, and the paper now says so.

Removed the binomial lower bounds added to Table 14 last round. Ten entries are
five runs times two classes over two physical acquisitions; attaching an interval
to them is pseudo-replication. A gate fails if they come back.

Narrowed: class exclusion has no reject option; five-seed results are
descriptive; run-level SDs are computational, not physical, replication; the
paired bootstrap preserves pairing and makes no parametric assumption but does
not remove dependence; disjoint-support counts are conservative reference counts,
never an effective sample size; every per-hour alarm figure comes from a
316-window segment, about 10.5 minutes, and says so.

Title now "A Safety-Oriented Evaluation Protocol for Learned Gas-Monitoring

Laboratory MOX Benchmark". Keywords drop "sensor degradation".

Fourth round: self-audit
------------------------
Three rounds of corrections introduced defects of their own. Two are worth
naming. Round two added binomial lower bounds to Table 14's episode counts; round
three removed them because ten entries over two physical acquisitions cannot
carry an interval. And the round-three corrections were applied to tables but not
to the prose quoting them: the research-question table still said the miss-bound
ratio was 40 after it moved to 86, and six posteriors quoted inline were left at
their pre-rho values.

The cause was structural. verify_v13.py compared table cells to result files and
nothing compared the document to itself, so a number in prose disagreeing with
the table it quotes, one table disagreeing with another, or a value a previous
revision replaced surviving into this one, were all unguarded.

self_audit.py closes that: 989 checks, none of which reads a result file. Seven
families, the blunt one being a list of eighteen strings that were once true and
are now wrong, which fails if any reappears anywhere. verify_v13.py now also
checks every posterior named in prose against the artifact.

quotes, boundary handling at a = 2 against a = 3, the burden conversion, and a
two-sided embargo on every seed and class.
